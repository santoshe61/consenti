## 2. Findings requiring attention

### Finding: UK PECR cookie-consent exemptions under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025

- **Jurisdiction / regulation:** UK-GDPR / PECR
- **Source:** Stevens & Bolton LLP analysis of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — retrieved 2026-07-30 — effective 5 February 2026 (already in force as of this review's run date)
- **What changed:** Three categories of cookies are now exempt from PECR's prior-consent requirement, provided the operator gives clear information and a free opt-out: (1) first-party-only statistical/analytics cookies (data used only by the site operator, not shared for advertising), (2) appearance/functionality preference cookies (e.g. accessibility, display settings), and (3) emergency-assistance cookies (e.g. location for emergency services). Cookies whose data is shared with third parties for advertising remain fully subject to the existing consent requirement — the exemption is narrow, not a general loosening.
- **Impact classification:** Uncertain — needs human legal review (with a low-risk docs-only path already available — see below)
- **Suggested change (draft only — not applied):**
  - Code: N/A. Consenti's existing `legalBasis: 'legitimate_interest'` option (`packages/utils/src/compliance.ts` — `COOKIE_PURPOSE_DEFAULTS`, already the default for the `functional` and `preferences` purposes) already implements a "notify, don't block, allow objection" pattern that maps reasonably well onto the new exemption's "clear info + free opt-out" requirement. No new legal-basis type appears strictly necessary on a first read.
  - Docs: `apps/docs/src/app/docs/compliance/uk-gdpr/page.tsx` — add a section describing the DUAA cookie exemptions and note that a UK operator relying on the exemption for a first-party-only analytics or preferences category can classify it `legalBasis: 'legitimate_interest'` instead of `'consent'` in their profile, rather than treating every non-necessary UK cookie as requiring the full opt-in flow.
  - Behavior-change risk: None if scoped to docs only — this doesn't change any default, just documents an option operators can already reach. The "needs human legal review" flag is because whether `legitimate_interest`'s existing notify+object UX (banner text + toggle, not a dedicated "opt-out link" pattern) actually satisfies DUAA's specific "free opt-out" wording is a legal judgment call, not something this run can resolve — a human/counsel should confirm before the docs page asserts equivalence as fact rather than "a reasonable starting point."

### Finding: CCPA/CPRA — ADMT (Automated Decision-Making Technology) opt-out rights, effective 2026-01-01

- **Jurisdiction / regulation:** CCPA / CPRA
- **Source:** CPPA regulation index pages (`cppa.ca.gov/regulations/`) + secondary corroboration (Osano) — retrieved 2026-07-30
- **What changed:** New CPPA regulations require businesses using ADMT to make "significant decisions" about consumers to provide notice before the decision, honor opt-out requests, disclose purpose/method/outcome on request, and grant appeal rights.
- **Impact classification:** No product impact
- **Suggested change (draft only — not applied):** None. ADMT opt-out is a distinct data-subject-rights mechanism for automated decision-making (e.g. algorithmic lending or hiring decisions) — it isn't a cookie/tracking-consent flow, and Consenti doesn't perform ADMT decisioning. Noting this so a future reviewer doesn't mistake "California added an opt-out right" for something Consenti's existing opt-out cookie category should absorb — it's a separate obligation entirely, outside this product's scope.

### Finding: CPRA — "neural data" added to the sensitive-personal-information definition (SB 1223, effective 2026-01-01)

- **Jurisdiction / regulation:** CPRA
- **Source:** Multiple corroborating secondary sources (Hunton, Arnold & Porter, Osano) — retrieved 2026-07-30. Primary CPPA regulation PDF not directly fetched this run (see UNVERIFIED note above)
- **What changed:** California's definition of "sensitive personal information" now explicitly includes neural data (information generated by measuring activity of a consumer's central/peripheral nervous system, when used to make inferences).
- **Impact classification:** Docs-only update (low priority)
- **Suggested change (draft only — not applied):**
  - Code: N/A. `packages/utils/src/compliance.ts:187` — `CPRA_CATEGORIES`'s existing `'sensitive'` entry description ("geolocation, health, race, biometrics, etc.") already generically covers this via "etc.," and the mechanism (opt-in required even in California for anything tagged `cpraCategory: 'sensitive'`) needs no change.
  - Docs: Could add "neural data" explicitly to the `'sensitive'` category's example list for currency, but this is optional polish, not a gap — skip unless bundled with other CPRA docs work.
  - Behavior-change risk: None.

### Finding: Brazil–EU mutual adequacy decision (2026-01-26)

- **Jurisdiction / regulation:** LGPD
- **Source:** International Network of Privacy Law Professionals (INPLP), Brazil IP counsel (mjzanon.com) — retrieved 2026-07-30. `gov.br/anpd` not directly fetched (see UNVERIFIED note above)
- **What changed:** Brazil and the EU adopted mutual adequacy decisions (ANPD Resolution CD/ANPD No. 32/2026 + the corresponding EU implementing decision), allowing personal data to move between Brazil and the EU without additional transfer mechanisms (SCCs etc.) under LGPD Art. 33(I).
- **Impact classification:** No product impact
- **Suggested change (draft only — not applied):** None required. This removes a transfer-mechanism burden between two jurisdictions; it doesn't change consent-capture requirements. Optional: the LGPD page could mention EU is now an adequate destination if/when the page gets other LGPD updates — not worth a standalone docs PR for this alone.

### Finding: APPI (Japan) amendment — promulgated 2026-07-17, not yet in force

- **Jurisdiction / regulation:** APPI
- **Source:** Mori Hamada & Matsumoto, Fisher Phillips — retrieved 2026-07-30
- **What changed:** An APPI amendment bill passed the Diet 2026-07-10 and was promulgated 2026-07-17. It introduces direct administrative monetary penalties (currently enforcement relies on corrective orders), a new consent exemption allowing collection/sharing of publicly available sensitive personal data for statistical/AI-training purposes under safeguards, and a children's-data provision requiring parental consent for children under 16 (raised from no APPI-specific age threshold today) with enhanced deletion/suspension rights for children. **Effective date is not yet set** — a cabinet order will fix it, no later than July 2028.
- **Impact classification:** Compliance-group default change (future — not actionable yet)
- **Suggested change (draft only — not applied):**
  - Code: `packages/utils/src/compliance.ts:698` (Japan → `general-privacy-consent`) — no change now. When the cabinet order sets an effective date, the under-16 parental-consent threshold would need to reach APPI's regulation page and, if Consenti wants a first-class age-gate default for Japan (the way DPDPA has one), a `dpdpa`-style dedicated profile block might be worth modeling then — not before the requirement is actually in force.
  - Docs: `apps/docs/src/app/docs/compliance/appi/page.tsx` — worth a forward-looking callout now ("an amendment changing the children's-consent age to under-16 has been promulgated but is not yet in force; watch for the implementing cabinet order") so operators aren't caught flat-footed, even though no functional change is due yet.
  - Behavior-change risk: N/A until in force. **Carry forward to next quarter's review** — check whether the cabinet order has set an effective date.

## 4. Out of scope, flagged anyway

- **EU Digital Omnibus (GDPR/ePrivacy):** A live legislative proposal that would move cookie-consent rules into the GDPR itself (new Articles 88a/88b — single-click accept/reject, a six-month re-ask moratorium after refusal, browser-level consent signals made legally binding) and reduce the share of cookies requiring consent by an estimated 60%. As of the June 2026 Council compromise text, the cookie-specific articles were **removed** from the negotiating text after member states couldn't agree — final adoption (if any) isn't expected before late 2026. Not in scope for this run (GDPR itself is unchanged), but this is the single most product-relevant thing to watch going into Q4 — if it lands with browser-level signals made binding, that's directly adjacent to Consenti's existing GPC handling.
- **Turkey KVKK — draft AI-generated-content liability amendment:** Submitted to the Grand National Assembly 2026-01-09. Addresses platform liability for unauthorised AI-generated audio/visual/written content, not cookie/tracking consent — doesn't affect Consenti's KVKK mode even if enacted. Noted for completeness only.
- **Thailand PDPA — PDPC guidelines consultation:** A public hearing on six priority guideline areas (legal bases, breach notification, DPO obligations, direct marketing, records of processing, CCTV/national-ID data collection) ran 2026-04-01–02, following March 2026 stakeholder engagement. Guidelines not yet finalized — worth checking next quarter once published, since the "marketing and direct marketing" and "legal bases for processing" areas could touch consent-capture requirements.

## 5. Summary for public comment

This is Consenti's first quarterly compliance-currency review, covering the 10 Maintained/Supported-tier
jurisdictions from 2026-01-01 (project baseline) through 2026-07-30. The most concrete, already-in-force
finding is the **UK's new PECR cookie exemptions** (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, effective 5 Feb 2026):
first-party-only analytics and preference/appearance cookies no longer strictly require prior consent if
paired with clear info and a free opt-out. Consenti likely already has the mechanism to support this
(`legalBasis: 'legitimate_interest'`), so the proposed action is a **docs-only** addition to the UK-GDPR
page, not a code or default change — but the "does our existing legitimate-interest UX legally satisfy
DUAA's opt-out wording" question needs a human/counsel sign-off before the docs assert that as settled fact.
Two lower-priority docs-only items (CPRA's neural-data addition to "sensitive" PI, and Thailand PDPA's
newly formalized Binding Corporate Rules mechanism) are proposed alongside it. One finding — an APPI
(Japan) amendment lowering the child-consent age to under-16 — is promulgated but not yet in force and
needs no action until an effective date is set; it's carried forward for next quarter. One claim (Quebec
Law 25 penalty/DPIA "2026 amendments" reported by secondary sources) could not be corroborated against the
CAI's own site and needs a human to track down the primary source before it's treated as real. Everything
else scanned clean — no in-window change found, or the change found was too old to be in scope, or (EU
Digital Omnibus, Turkey's AI-content bill) still at the proposal stage.
